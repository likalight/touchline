One moment, every fan

A stadium in your browser

The match plays in a floodlit 3D stadium: animated players in club kits, a crowd that jumps when their team scores, LED boards that carry the latest checked insight, a replay angle on every goal and a camera that can follow one player all match.

Four voices

The same goal is written for a casual fan, an analyst, a studio lower-third and a listener who can't see the screen. English, Spanish, French and Portuguese work offline. Any other language comes from Microsoft Foundry, and is fact-checked again.

Stories that last ninety minutes

A storyline agent remembers the whole match. When Varga scores in the 44th minute, it remembers that his loss of possession gave away a goal in the 28th, and says so. Its numbers are recounted from the raw events before anything airs.

Inspect the evidence

Open a live insight's receipt to see its source events, checked fields and model contributions. The receipt states its scope: a source check is not proof of real-world accuracy or causality.

Graphics that keep time

Each moment becomes a cue keyed to the match clock, with a layer, a priority and copy in every language. A producer can approve it first. The graphics template loads in CasparCG and SPX, the open playout tools broadcasters use.

Your match, your reel

After the final whistle, every fan gets their own highlights: their club, the players they follow, in their language, replayed in the 3D stadium with the storyline written in, and saved straight from the browser.

Agents that check each other

A Microsoft Agent Framework workflow runs per moment: an analyst, a fact-checker, four narrators side by side, then a producer that decides what deserves the screen and says why. Every handoff is traced, and the live show never waits on a model.

Break it on purpose

Corrupt the feed, take the language model down, crash an agent or slip in a made-up number, while the match plays. The log shows which agent caught each fault and how long recovery took. Building these drills found a real gap in our own fact-checker, now fixed.

An experiment in pixels

A separate prototype extracts candidate passes and ball speed from rendered synthetic video. Its calibrated camera and limited ball detection do not establish accuracy on real broadcast footage.
